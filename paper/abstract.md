**Building an Auditable Literature Evidence Pipeline: Corpus Quality, Retrieval, and Structured Extraction for Graph Neural Networks**

Automated research-gap discovery is often treated as a generation problem, but its outputs are only as trustworthy as three earlier components: whether the literature corpus is genuinely on-topic, whether relevant prior work can be retrieved, and whether extracted claims are accurate and traceable to their source. This work reports a staged, evidence-oriented evaluation of a literature-analysis pipeline for graph neural network (GNN) research. We first identify and correct a corpus contamination failure, where naive keyword search admitted thousands of unrelated papers, and rebuild a verified 1,951-paper corpus using a stricter, phrase-qualified inclusion rule. We then benchmark hybrid retrieval (BM25, dense embeddings, and their fusion) against citation-derived relevance from 19 literature surveys, and evaluate structured LLM-based claim extraction with mandatory verbatim evidence spans across the full corpus. A 76-paper human-annotated validation shows that naive exact-match scoring severely underestimates extraction quality (0% vs. 75–90% under semantic matching), revealing evaluation methodology itself as a source of error. An extended attempt at statistical gap detection is reported as a negative result, with corpus-scale requirements identified as the likely cause. The work establishes a reproducible, auditable foundation for literature-based discovery while explicitly delineating what remains unvalidated.
